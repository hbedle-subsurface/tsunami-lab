# Instructor notes

## Time and fit

- About 1 to 1.5 hours of student work. Quick students finish in under an hour.
- Built for a laptop: the panel and the instructions sit side by side and fit on the screen. A tablet works too. A phone works but is cramped.
- Every answer is typed or chosen, so the whole lab can be done with a keyboard. The panels are visual, so a student who uses a screen reader would need an accommodation (the text of each step and the scenario card carry the key facts).
- Each step has a "Stuck, or something not working?" box covering lost progress, pop-up blockers, and sending you a Canvas message with their scenario number.
- No login and no data leaves the student's browser. Progress is saved in that browser, and a student can get the same evacuation scenario back on another computer with their name and scenario number.

## Course learning outcomes it supports

- **Seismic risk assessment (tsunamis)** - how tsunamis form, travel and grow; local vs. distant tsunamis (steps 1 to 3).
- **Interrelationships of plate tectonics, volcanoes and earthquakes** - tsunamis from megathrust earthquakes, volcanic collapse and explosions (steps 3, 4).
- **Interdisciplinary connections** - wave physics, kinetic energy, rates and unit conversions, logarithmic scales (steps 1, 2, 5, 6).
- **Society intersections** - warning systems, the 2022 Tonga surprise, evacuation planning and vertical evacuation structures (steps 3, 4, 6).

## Grading

Students reach the certificate only after every step is correct, so grading can be complete/incomplete.

To check a certificate, open `verify.html` (linked at the bottom of the lab), type the student's name exactly as on the certificate and the five-digit scenario number. The page shows the code the certificate should have and that student's evacuation scenario with the correct answers. The code is a light check, not a secure signature.

## Suggested Canvas assignment text

> **Lab: Race the Wave**
>
> Open the lab at https://hbedle-subsurface.github.io/tsunami-lab/ and enter your name exactly as it appears in Canvas. Work through all six steps. Each step checks your answers, and there are hints if you get stuck. When you finish, save your certificate as a PDF or take a screenshot of the whole certificate, and upload it here.
>
> Plan on about an hour to an hour and a half. Your progress is saved in your browser, so you can stop and come back, but use the same computer and browser to finish.

## Publishing on GitHub Pages (web interface)

1. On github.com, create a new public repository named `tsunami-lab` under hbedle-subsurface.
2. Click **Add file > Upload files**, drag in everything from this folder (keep the `css` and `js` folders), and commit.
3. Go to **Settings > Pages**. Set Source to **Deploy from a branch**, Branch to **main** and folder to **/ (root)**, and save.
4. After a minute or two the lab is live at https://hbedle-subsurface.github.io/tsunami-lab/

The GoatCounter page-view counter (`hbedle`) is already in `index.html`.
